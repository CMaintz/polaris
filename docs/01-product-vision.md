# Product Vision

## Problem

LinkedIn users accumulate posts, reactions, comments, audience signals and performance data, but the platform does not provide a coherent intelligence layer for answering questions such as:

- What is actually working?
- Which topics resonate with my audience?
- How has my content changed over time?
- What should I publish next?
- Which experiments are worth running?
- Is a change in performance meaningful?
- How do external events or trends relate to my content?

Polaris turns fragmented information into an understandable operating picture.

## Core value proposition

Polaris should answer:

> "Given what has happened, what does it mean, what should I consider doing next, and why?"

## Design principles

### Evidence before advice

Recommendations should be grounded in observed data.

### Context over generic AI

The AI should know the user's actual history, goals and audience context.

### User agency

The system supports decisions rather than pretending that one universal strategy exists.

### Progressive automation

Automation is introduced only after reliable measurement and review workflows exist.

### Provider independence

AI, social platforms and external intelligence sources are replaceable.

### Privacy by design

Collect the minimum information required and make data flows explicit.

### Explainability

Users should be able to inspect the evidence behind important AI conclusions.

## Success criteria

The product should make it materially easier to:

1. understand account performance
2. understand content performance
3. identify patterns
4. form hypotheses
5. plan content
6. run experiments
7. evaluate results
8. maintain a coherent long-term strategy
